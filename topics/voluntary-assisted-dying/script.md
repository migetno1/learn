# Voluntary Assisted Dying — Presentation Script

> Status: developed teaching draft. NSW law and public service details rechecked 12 July 2026. Legal and clinical review is still required before presentation. This script is educational and is not individual medical or legal advice.

## Audience and format

- Primary audience: GP registrars and practising GPs.
- Secondary audience: senior medical students.
- Core duration: approximately 35–38 minutes.
- Optional discussion: 7–10 minutes, removable without disrupting the core conclusion.
- Jurisdiction: New South Wales, with a Bathurst and Western NSW focus.
- Delivery: full-viewport scrollytelling with keyboard and fullscreen enhancements. Slow, reversible reveals replay when the presenter scrolls back down. All content remains understandable without motion.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, learners should be able to:

1. Apply the NSW eligibility criteria as conjunctive statutory requirements rather than a diagnostic shortcut.
2. Recognise when a patient’s words constitute a first request under s 19.
3. State the receiving medical practitioner’s immediate, two-business-day and five-business-day duties.
4. Explain VAD-specific decision-making capacity and why dementia or mental illness neither automatically qualifies nor excludes a person.
5. Offer a verified Bathurst navigation pathway while continuing ordinary clinical care.

## Clinical vignette

Margaret is a fictional 72-year-old Bathurst patient with metastatic pancreatic cancer. Her oncologist expects that she is likely to die within six months. She has mild cognitive impairment and takes antidepressant medication. Her daughter is present.

Margaret says:

> “I’ve thought about this carefully. I want to request voluntary assisted dying.”

Her daughter adds:

> “This is what Mum wants.”

The facts are deliberately incomplete. The vignette must not imply that Margaret is eligible; only authorised coordinating and consulting practitioners can make the statutory assessments. [S1; S2]

## Scene sequence and presenter notes

### 1. Opening — When a patient asks about VAD (2 minutes)

Display the NSW jurisdiction and “current as at” date before presenting any legal rule.

Spoken frame:

> “This is a NSW presentation. The Act is the controlling authority; NSW Health guidance explains implementation. Other Australian jurisdictions differ.”

### 2. The consultation (4 minutes)

Reveal the vignette. Ask before explaining:

1. Has a formal first request been made?
2. What must the GP do before deciding whether Margaret is eligible?
3. What legal significance should be given to the cognitive and psychiatric diagnoses?

Teaching point: Margaret’s own words appear capable of being a clear and unambiguous request made during a consultation. Her daughter’s statement is not the request and cannot substitute for Margaret’s voluntariness or capacity. [S1, s 19]

### 3. Eligibility is a conjunction (7 minutes)

Display all s 16 criteria together:

- adult status;
- the Australian citizenship, permanent-residency or three-year residence limb;
- ordinary NSW residence for at least 12 months, subject to the statutory exemption pathway;
- an advanced, progressive condition that will cause death;
- prognosis of six months, or 12 months for a neurodegenerative condition;
- suffering that cannot be relieved in a way the person considers tolerable;
- VAD-specific decision-making capacity;
- voluntariness, absence of pressure or duress, and an enduring request. [S1, s 16]

Then emphasise:

> “Disability, dementia and mental health impairment are not sufficient by themselves. They are also not automatic exclusions.”

Do not invite the audience to pronounce Margaret eligible. Ask which facts require formal assessment and verification.

### 4. The first request changes the clock (9 minutes)

#### Trigger

A first request must be the patient’s clear and unambiguous request to a medical practitioner, during a medical consultation. It may be verbal or communicated another way. General enquiries are not first requests. [S1, s 19; S6]

#### Receiving practitioner duties

1. Decide to accept or refuse. A practitioner who is not eligible to coordinate must immediately refuse. [S1, s 21]
2. Within two business days, communicate acceptance or refusal and give the approved information, unless refusing because of conscientious objection. [S1, s 21; S4]
3. If refusing because of conscientious objection, communicate the refusal immediately. [S1, s 21(5)]
4. Record the request, decision, reason for refusal where applicable, and information-giving status. [S1, s 22]
5. Complete and submit the First Request Form to the Board within five business days after the decision, whether accepting or refusing. [S1, s 23; S4]

Clarify that conscientious objection permits refusal to participate but does not erase the residual first-request duties or permit withdrawal of ordinary care. Offering a Care Navigator connection is practical continuity guidance, not an express statutory requirement to refer to a named clinician. [S1, ss 9, 21–23 and 28(1)(k); S4; S10]

### 5. Capacity — assess the decision, not the diagnosis (8 minutes)

Display the s 6 elements:

- understand required information or advice;
- remember it to the extent necessary;
- understand the matters involved and the effect of the decision;
- weigh the relevant factors;
- communicate the decision in some way. [S1, s 6]

Teaching sequence:

1. Begin with the statutory presumption of capacity.
2. Assess the particular VAD decision at the relevant time.
3. Support communication and consider fluctuation.
4. Ask what effect the cognitive or psychiatric condition actually has on the statutory abilities, voluntariness and absence of pressure.
5. If an authorised assessor cannot decide a relevant eligibility matter, use the statutory opinion-referral process. [S1, ss 26–27; S2]

Return to Margaret. Her mild cognitive impairment and antidepressant use are prompts for careful assessment, not legal conclusions.

### 6. Bathurst pathway (5 minutes)

Present a service pathway rather than a named practitioner:

1. Complete the GP’s first-request duties if triggered and continue ordinary care.
2. Western NSW LHD VAD Navigation Service: **1800 684 283**, Monday–Friday, **9 am–3 pm**, excluding public holidays. [S8]
3. NSW VAD Care Navigator Service: **1300 802 133**, Monday–Friday, **8:30 am–4:30 pm**, excluding public holidays; **NSLHD-VADCareNavigator@health.nsw.gov.au**. [S7]
4. Continue GP, specialist, palliative, symptom, psychosocial, cultural and mental-health care.
5. Do not promise eligibility, practitioner availability, timing or an entirely telehealth pathway. IPTAAS may assist if its criteria are met. [S2; S3; S14]

### 7. Core close — the first-request checklist (2 minutes)

1. Recognise the trigger.
2. Respond within the statutory timeframes.
3. Record and notify.
4. Assess capacity rather than diagnostic labels.
5. Continue care and offer a verified navigation pathway.

The presentation may end here.

### 8. Optional discussion — Misconceptions on trial (7–10 minutes)

Mark this section visibly as optional. For each proposition, obtain a preliminary audience verdict before revealing the answer.

1. “Any request to die is a formal VAD first request.” — Incorrect; clarify whether the s 19 trigger is met. [S1; S4; S9]
2. “Dementia or mental illness automatically excludes a person.” — Incorrect; neither diagnosis is determinative. Every criterion, including capacity, must be assessed. [S1; S2]
3. “A GP can ignore a first request if they conscientiously object.” — Incorrect; immediate refusal, documentation and Board notification remain required. [S1; S4]
4. “A family member or advance care directive can request VAD.” — Incorrect; only the patient can request it and must retain VAD-specific capacity. [S1; S13]

## Content boundaries

- Do not include drug names, doses, preparation or administration instructions.
- Do not reproduce restricted portal, prescription or practitioner-list material.
- Do not state or imply that the fictional patient is eligible.
- Do not promise access, approval, timing, cost or location.
- Distinguish statutory rules, NSW Health implementation guidance, professional obligations and teaching interpretation.
