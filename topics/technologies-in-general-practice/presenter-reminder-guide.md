## 1 — Technology should help the patient

- **Open:** “This is not a catalogue of impressive tools.”
- **Question:** Does it improve care in the real workflow?
- Cover each time: exact task → evidence → our practice → barriers.
- Distinguish **current / emerging / research horizon**.
- Optional show of hands: scribe, evidence assistant, other AI tool?
- **Key line:** A polished output is not a patient outcome.
- **Transition:** Meet the person before the technology.

## 2 — Meet Michael

- Fictional, 64, self-employed school-bus driver, outside town, patchy internet.
- Books for frequency + nocturia; known BPH creates anchoring risk.
- Wider story: thirst, fatigue, poor sleep, route safety.
- Wants concise written plan + teach-back + fewer trips.
- Can refuse technology; phone/paper/ordinary care remain available.
- Ordinary assessment later identifies type 2 diabetes—**no AI diagnoses him**.
- Ask silently throughout: benefit to Michael, or work/risk shifted to him?

## 3 — Adaptive pre-consultation history

- **Task/how:** patient completes adaptive questionnaire; GP receives patient-entered structured summary.
- **Patient:** private, self-paced way to remember and express concerns.
- **Doctor:** sees broader agenda early; must still re-take and verify important history.
- **Evidence:** current platform; independent patient-outcome benefit not established. Trial protocol ≠ effectiveness result.
- **Our practice:** target complex/new/chronic-review appointments; decide who checks responses and urgency.
- **Pitfalls:** non-completion, literacy, language, disability, data cost, internet, summary loses nuance.
- **Must keep:** phone, paper and ordinary consultation.
- **Key line:** Widen the doorway; do not make the form the price of entry.

## 4 — Ambient scribe

- **Task/how:** consent → audio → speech recognition → language-model summary → draft → GP review/correction → authorised record.
- **Patient:** possible better eye contact; may disclose less if uncomfortable with recording.
- **Doctor:** potentially less typing; still checks every clinically important element.
- **Evidence:** promising workflow/wellbeing signals; manual editing common; safety and Australian patient-outcome evidence limited. Effects vary by tool.
- **Our practice:** small opt-in pilot; standard consent; audit omissions, fabricated examination, medicine/allergy errors and correction time; ask patients.
- **Pitfalls:** mishearing, omission, fabrication, chronology, attribution, boilerplate, downstream copy-forward.
- **Governance:** data location/retention/training; local policy; jurisdiction-specific recording law; MDO/legal advice.
- **Check Michael:** thirst, fatigue, sleep, route safety; no invented examination.
- **Key line:** Draft, not record. Clinician remains the author.

## 5 — Evidence-linked prompts

- **Task/how:** structured record data + transparent rule/risk logic → point-of-care prompt or worklist.
- **Patient:** may not touch system; missing monitoring/prevention becomes visible.
- **Doctor/team:** checks source data and whether prompt applies; owns action.
- **Evidence:** Australian feasibility and implementation evidence; outcome depends on rule + data + workflow.
- **Our practice:** choose one high-value care gap; trace identify → verify → contact → act → close.
- **Pitfalls:** incomplete coding, missing external results, stale data, false positives/negatives, alert fatigue.
- **Key line:** A useful care gap is accurate, actionable and owned.

## 6 — Emerging generative second check

- **Task/how:** model analyses entered encounter data; gives constrained diagnostic/treatment feedback inside workflow.
- **Safe sequence:** independent assessment → AI feedback → verify → clinician decides.
- **Patient:** indirect safety-net benefit; not diagnosed by chatbot.
- **Evidence:** AI Consult Kenyan trial, 9,691 patients; no significant improvement in primary 14-day treatment-failure outcome; documentation quality improved. Different setting. GPT-4 access vignette trial did not improve clinician reasoning.
- **Our practice:** watch/evaluate, not public-chatbot adoption. Demand intended purpose, ARTG status if applicable, local evidence, integration, audit and escalation.
- **Pitfalls:** persuasive wrong answer, silence mistaken for safety, bad inputs, automation bias, poor population transfer.
- **Key line:** A capable model is not a safe clinical system.

## 7 — Evidence retrieval

- **Task/how:** search curated corpus → retrieve passages → generate synthesis + source links.
- **Patient:** better shared-decision conversation, not a pasted answer.
- **Doctor:** frame “works” → open citation → check population/comparator/outcomes/follow-up → discuss priorities.
- **Evidence:** MedLuma current Australian example; independent product accuracy/utility/outcome evidence not identified.
- **Our practice:** compare a non-identifiable question with normal search; assess key-source retrieval and accurate representation.
- **Pitfalls:** missing source, partial citation support, outdated corpus, flattened disagreement, citation theatre.
- **Do not:** enter identifiable data or give an unresearched PAE number.
- **Key line:** Use it to find evidence; its answer is not the evidence.

## 8 — Patient-information drafting

- **Task/how:** authoritative sources + defined audience/action → draft → claim check → accessibility/consumer review → clinician approval → teach-back.
- **Patient:** concise plan in preferred format; still checks understanding with GP.
- **Doctor:** faster first draft and alternate wording; owns every claim.
- **Evidence:** readability/satisfaction promising; accuracy, completeness and actionability mixed; mostly English research.
- **Our practice:** choose one common low-risk handout; controlled sources, safety-netting, version control and update owner.
- **Pitfalls:** lost qualifier, invented fact, ambiguous action, outdated link, update burden, patronising “simplification”.
- **Language:** professional interpreter for complex communication/consent; AI translation is not a substitute.
- **Key line:** Controlled editorial workflow, not automatic publication.

## 9 — Follow-up, reminders and recalls

- **Task/how now:** eligibility rules + structured record → worklist → human verification/contact/action/closure.
- **Future:** tailored two-way messages, simple questions, booking and exception escalation.
- **Patient:** chooses channel; can correct data or decline optional care.
- **Team:** distinguish prompt vs reminder vs clinically important recall.
- **Evidence:** identification/reminders current; safe semi-autonomous closed-loop outreach less mature.
- **Our practice:** audit one pathway end-to-end; name owner, timeframe, exception and fail-safe.
- **Pitfalls:** wrong person, wrong eligibility, coercive repetition, digital exclusion, reply nobody reads, hidden reception/nursing workload.
- **Key line:** Finding the patient is not closure.

## 10 — Remote monitoring

- **Task/how:** patient enters device/symptom data → service receives it → thresholds or emerging models prioritise change → team responds.
- **Patient:** less travel; must know exactly when monitoring occurs and what to do when unwell/disconnected.
- **Doctor/team:** defined inclusion, review frequency, triggers, after-hours plan, documentation and end date.
- **Evidence:** Australian monitoring services exist; reliable AI prediction and autonomous response remain emerging.
- **Our practice:** only for a defined problem with funded response capacity; begin time-limited.
- **Pitfalls:** false alarms, missing data, anxiety, device/connectivity inequity, unclear ownership, false belief of continuous watching.
- **Key line:** Design responsibility before collecting data.

## 11 — Individual treatment optimisation

- **Task/how:** estimate difference in benefit/harm between reasonable treatments for one person; show uncertainty.
- **Patient + GP:** use output inside shared decision-making; no autonomous prescription.
- **Evidence:** PERSONAL antiseizure-medicine trial makes direction credible; does not establish general GP or diabetes system.
- **Our practice:** no adoption proposal; learn what evidence to demand.
- Ask: treatment effect or prognosis? External validation? Calibration? Representative population? Real workflow outcome?
- **Pitfalls:** false precision, biased data, poor transfer, obsolete model, patient preference reduced to a variable.
- **Key line:** Personalised prediction should strengthen—not replace—preference.

## 12 — Narrow retinal image AI

- **Task/how:** retinal image → task-specific classifier → image-quality/result pathway → confirmation/referral.
- **Patient:** screening closer to practice; ungradable ≠ negative.
- **Doctor/team:** acquisition, quality, communication, confirmatory assessment and referral.
- **Evidence:** Australian GP glaucoma study: 414 recruited, 277 analysable; sensitivity 65.0%, specificity 94.6%; acquisition was a major limitation. Do not transfer figures to diabetic-retinopathy screening.
- **Our practice:** first map exact condition, device/intended purpose, regulation, equipment/training, reimbursement and referral capacity.
- **Pitfalls:** ungradable images, false reassurance, cost, operator skill, group performance, referral bottleneck.
- **Key line:** The pathway—not only the classifier—determines benefit.

## 13 — Clinician-learning appendix (optional)

### Diagnostic brainstorming

- Fictional/de-identified case only.
- Independent reasoning → synthetic challenge → authoritative verification.
- Benefit: cognitive forcing; possible omitted diagnosis.
- Evidence: public LLM access has not shown improved physician reasoning in cited trial.
- Pitfalls: anchoring, rare-disease noise, invented facts, extra tests, privacy breach.
- Practice use: education session, not undocumented patient-specific second opinion.

### AI-assisted Anki

- Selected source → draft one-point cards → check facts/qualifiers/source → Anki schedules review.
- Evidence supports feasibility, not better competence or patient outcomes.
- Practice use: small, reviewed deck from an agreed guideline update.
- Pitfalls: plausible clutter, repeated misinformation, future maintenance.
- **Key line:** Challenge and draft; do not outsource understanding.

## 14 — Close

- **System may:** capture, retrieve, classify, prioritise, draft, monitor.
- **Patient + clinician still:** choose, interpret, verify, decide, respond, remain accountable.
- Three possible practice conversations:
  - Standardise consent + error audit for any scribe trial.
  - Choose one care-gap/recall pathway and give it ownership through closure.
  - Trial one source-grounded, reviewed patient-information workflow.
- Six closing questions: exact task? maturity? workflow evidence? data? refusal? failure/fallback?
- **Final line:** “Define the task. Appraise the workflow. Protect choice and data. Keep a fallback. Own the consequence.”
- **Invite discussion:** Most useful here? What hidden workload or risk must be solved first?

## If challenged on the evidence

- “The review was targeted, not systematic.”
- “Most claims are task-, product-, version- and workflow-specific.”
- “Vendor material describes function, not benefit.”
- “Australian evidence remains limited for several categories.”
- “A benchmark result or ARTG inclusion does not prove local clinical utility.”
- “The responsible next step is a defined pilot with measures and stop criteria—not broad adoption.”

## Safety lines worth saying verbatim

- “No AI makes Michael’s diagnosis.”
- “A scribe creates a draft, not a record.”
- “Citations help us check an answer; they do not make it correct.”
- “Remote monitoring does not mean continuous watching.”
- “An ungradable image is not a negative screen.”
- “The patient can refuse the technology and still receive ordinary care.”
