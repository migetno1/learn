# Technologies in General Practice — Learning Guide Plan

> **Planning status:** approved 12-recommendation architecture implemented as a patient-centred guide and presentation. Current as at 12 July 2026 (Australia). Outstanding clinical, legal/privacy, consumer and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander review remains explicit.

## Design brief

- **Narrative question:** When does technology genuinely help Michael?
- **Continuous case:** Michael, 64, a self-employed school-bus driver living outside town, with known BPH, a broader symptom story and a later ordinary diagnosis of type 2 diabetes.
- **Hero:** full-viewport editorial image with Michael’s journey framed against a technology landscape, using the mist-blue topic accent.
- **Motion:** the learning guide uses restrained opacity-plus-transform entrances and a subtle hero drift. The presentation gives each semantic content unit a directional enter/rest/exit lifecycle as scenes pass the viewport; reduced motion renders every unit immediately in its fully visible rest state.
- **Interaction:** no teaching content depends on clicking. Links, fullscreen and presentation navigation are utilities.
- **Visual rhythm:** editorial Michael art, workflow bands, evidence boundaries, dark interludes, source-native diagrams and alternating presentation compositions.

## Implemented guide architecture

1. Meet Michael before technology.
2. Before the consultation: adaptive pre-consultation history.
3. During the consultation: ambient documentation.
4. During the consultation: evidence-linked prompts.
5. During the consultation: emerging generative second check.
6. After the consultation: evidence retrieval.
7. After the consultation: reviewed patient information and health-literacy adaptation.
8. Between consultations: preventive identification and future outreach.
9. Between consultations: remote and longitudinal monitoring.
10. Future treatment: individual treatment-effect optimisation.
11. Narrow AI-assisted retinal screening.
12. Clinician-learning appendix: diagnostic brainstorming and Anki.

Each technology section explicitly states the task, how it works, workflow effect, patient benefit, application to Michael, evidence/maturity, failure modes and clinician oversight.

## Evidence labels

- **Current:** available in or directly applicable to Australian general practice now.
- **Emerging:** prospective evaluation, limited deployment or early implementation.
- **Research horizon:** credible 3–10-year direction, not a care promise.
- **Boundary:** current product functionality or educational use without demonstrated clinical benefit.

## Michael safeguards

- No real patient is represented.
- Technology does not diagnose Michael.
- Michael can refuse, correct data and choose a non-digital pathway.
- Travel, connectivity and work pressures make remote or local care relevant without stereotyping.
- A concise written-plan preference is stated rather than inferred from age, postcode or diagnosis.
- The case contains no exact diabetes result, treatment recommendation or unresearched PAE statistic.

## Publication gates

1. Recheck product version, Australian availability, intended purpose, data flows and ARTG status where relevant.
2. Obtain clinical review of Michael’s case and all diabetes, screening and monitoring references.
3. Obtain legal/privacy or MDO review of recording, consent, data and automated-contact wording.
4. Obtain consumer and accessibility review.
5. Obtain appropriately authorised Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander review and governance where relevant.
6. Run the complete build and browser QA matrix before handoff.
